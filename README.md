# KnowDo 知行

**The Unity of Knowledge and Action.**

KnowDo is an app for doing what you said you would do — and keeping the books on it. Write down a task and how long it gets, tap ▶, and a countdown holds you to it. Drift onto something else and it notices and brings you back. At half-time and at the deadline, a full-screen page takes over and asks: can you still finish in time? Did you finish? If not, you extend the timer on the spot, or write down how you will finish. Every day, what you promised is lined up against what you actually did.

It is inspired by Wang Yangming's doctrine of the unity of knowledge and action: most people do not lack knowledge of what to do — they know, and still do not do it. KnowDo exists to find that gap the moment it opens and help close it.

**Who it is for — four depths of the same road.** Start at your own depth; the deeper layers wait for you:

- **Hold the brake** — a distraction-prone, easily scattered mind (ADHD-like, without pinning a diagnosis on anyone): external structure that helps you control phone use; the guard is an external brake, not behaviour control.
- **Keep it together** — efficiency-minded people who want the drifting to stop: no scattering between tasks, no floating during them, no time lost to scrolling apps.
- **Make it stand** — people building good habits: habits, the morning page and the Zhixing index turn “what I want to become” into a daily reckoning.
- **See it through** — people bringing self-cultivation into daily life: the two ledgers, principle companionship and daily reflection, so cultivation lands on each concrete task instead of stopping at reading and lectures.

**Quick start.** (1) Add one task for today and give it a length. (2) Tap ▶ — the countdown runs; put the phone down and work. (3) At half-time and at the end, answer the full-screen check: done, or extend and keep going. (4) When a task ends, pick what is next — never leave the gap empty; a timed rest counts too. (5) Tomorrow morning, fill the morning page first, then start the day. Everything else in this README and in the in-app Help is reference for when you need it.

> **Related project — [KnowDo for Mac](https://github.com/gty3310/knowdo-mac):** the macOS companion to KnowDo — a SwiftUI menu-bar and floating-window app that shares the same account, the same task list, and the same single cross-device timer with this Android app. Start a task on your phone and watch it count down on your Mac, or the other way around.

## Vision: two ledgers, three layers

KnowDo keeps two ledgers. **Knowing** is what you know, have promised, and aspire to do — your aspiration, principles, habits, diary, and every task you commit to. **Doing** is what actually happens — the timing, the completions, the drifts the guard catches, the self-checks you answer honestly. The product's one core job is to detect the gap between the two — “knowing without doing” — the moment it opens, put it in front of you, and help you close it.

The work is organised in three layers:

- **Practice (践诺)** — promises become timed action: the task table, countdowns, full-screen checks, the no-gap rule. This layer is fully built.
- **Reflection (省察)** — the daily and weekly reckoning: the morning page, self-checks, the Zhixing index, trend pages. Built, with room to deepen.
- **Conscience (良知)** — the long view: seeing which piece of “knowing” all your gaps jointly point to as not yet real. This is Yangming's own meaning of the unity of knowledge and action, and it is deliberately the thinnest layer today — the Know tab and the trend pages are its foundation, not its completion. KnowDo does not claim to have finished this layer.

The app's identity has changed four times on the way here: an external brain (task table + timer + full-screen checks), then a steward of all your time (the no-gap rule of v1.10.0 extended its reach from “during a task” to “between tasks, all day”), then a knowing–doing reckoning (the two ledgers and the Know system, v1.50.0 onwards), and most recently a health unity (sleep joins the books, v2.0.0). The direction has never changed: at any moment you know exactly what you should be doing — and the moment you drift, it notices and brings you back.

## How it works

Five mechanism groups carry the vision:

1. **Make “knowing” stand, and keep it visible.** Your aspiration appears exactly where resolve wobbles — the drift inquiry page, the end-of-task check page, the pause call-back page. The Know tab gathers aspiration, thinking principles, conduct principles, habits, open questions and people into six cross-linked groups. A task can carry one main principle; the completion page then quietly asks whether you lived it — and answering “didn't consider it” withdraws the link and keeps it out of the statistics: a principle never thought of while doing the work does not count as knowledge you have.
2. **Turn promises into timed action.** The task table states every commitment plainly (deadline or repeat, length, priority, contexts). Starting stores an absolute end time, so the countdown cannot drift. Half-time and end checks force an in-the-moment answer; subtasks split big work into 5–10 minute pieces.
3. **Leave no gap.** The most dangerous moment is between tasks. At any moment there are only three states — timing, on a timed pause, or choosing the next task — and even sleep is a “Sleep” task holding its place. Unlocking the phone with nothing running asks what is next.
4. **Guard the present moment.** A focus guard watches which app you are in; a screen-content guard watches what is on the screen (screenshots are analyzed in memory only — never saved, never uploaded). Being caught starts with a self-check — “were you doing what you should be doing?” — and graces are gated by a breathing pause and by the purpose you stated for a pause. Since v1.98.0 the guard reads your last 3 hours of activity and separates real drift from false drift (briefly handling another important matter is a temporary task switch), with four leniency tiers between them; false alarms have a formal appeal channel, and your explanations are fed back into the judging.
5. **Reckon daily, look back weekly.** The morning page opens with meaning (your aspiration and a self-written line for tonight) before any numbers; yesterday's habits are answered one by one, and a morning diary is written before the day unlocks. The Zhixing index (habits 50% + task completion 35% + drift control 15%) lines a week of knowing and doing up into one account, with trend pages behind it. Since v2.0.0, sleep is booked too: how last night went, and what time you commit to tonight, confirmed face-to-face every morning, with a recovery score whose formula is fully public.

## Features (Android, v2.3.0)

Current version: **v2.3.0**.

- **Excel-like task table** — columns you can show or hide, resize, and edit in place: task name, context tags (multi-select, custom tags allowed), deadline (date picker, or daily repeat), planned duration, priority, planned start time, time used / remaining. Tap a row to expand it in place and act on it; a checkbox marks it done; the row currently being timed is highlighted.
- **Focus timer with full-screen check-ins** — a countdown per task, driven by a foreground service that stores an absolute end time, so the countdown does not drift if the process is killed; exact alarms back up the half-time and end points. At half-time and at the deadline, a full-screen page takes over — via full-screen intent from the lock screen, or an overlay path on an unlocked screen — asking whether you can still finish and whether you did. Not done? Add +50% / +5 / +10 / +15 minutes or a custom extension, or write down your plan for finishing.
- **Always-on floating window** — a small overlay (docked tab / mini card / full edit card) that always shows your current task and its remaining time and follows you across apps. Pause from it (with a "remind me in N minutes" menu), jump back to your assistant, or edit the task note in place without opening the app.
- **Focus guard** — each task can carry a focus rule: a natural-language description plus a structured block/allow app list and a primary app. While a timer runs, the foreground app is checked every few seconds (via usage access, with a faster recheck whenever the window changes); open something unrelated and a full-screen page asks "are you working on the right task?" — return to the task at once, switch to another task, or take a grace: block the app now, or keep it for 1 / 3 / 5 more minutes, after which the disposition you picked lands — quit the app, send it to the background, or block it behind a 3-minute countdown page with no early exit on the page itself. Three difficulty levels (Settings → Guards, since v1.83.0): Easy is the classic flow; Medium shows the false-alarm button only on the day's first inquiry for that task and gates every grace behind a breathing pause that doubles with each use that day (15s, capped at 120s; leaving the breathing page voids the grace); Hard replaces the false-alarm button with a written explanation judged by an AI — only an "agree" verdict lets you off — and rations grace to a small daily quota. Every grace choice records one honest drift event for the Zhixing index.
- **Per-task app lists** — for each task, your installed apps are sorted into white / grey tiers (since v1.94.0 there is no blacklist at all): whitelisted apps no longer trigger focus-rule inquiries during that task, and grey adds no new interruptions — nothing is convicted by list, app category or keywords any more; the guard judges screen content and semantics instead. Open "App list" in a task's details to see the two tiers and re-tier any app — hand edits are marked as yours and never overwritten by later generations. Since v1.84.0 the lists live only on the phone (a local file, never synced to the cloud); a fresh list is requested through a one-slot cloud mailbox and filed locally when it arrives. The master switch is Settings → Judging & lists → "Task app lists"; with it off, no requests are sent and no list-based white-tier release applies. (Automatic classification currently serves the author's own account only — see the section for new users below.)
- **Screen-content guard** — for the distractions the focus guard cannot see: shopping or short videos inside an allowed browser, or adult content. Opt-in, and on-device only. While a timer runs on an unlocked screen, screenshots are sampled at random intervals and screened on-device — ML Kit OCR (Chinese / Japanese / English), a small on-device image classifier (ONNX), and a local semantic model that scores the screen text against the current task (since v1.94.0, app category and keyword rules no longer convict directly). Clear passes and clear violations settle locally; the uncertain middle band escalates to a cloud judge (tried first whenever the phone is online, since v1.69.0), and only extracted text features ever leave the phone. Two consecutive hits (one, for adult content, if you set it so) raise the same inquiry page as the focus guard. Screenshots are analyzed in memory and destroyed immediately: never saved, never uploaded; only the verdict events sync. Screenshots come only from the accessibility channel (v1.26.0: the screen-recording channel was removed entirely — no recording permission is ever requested; with accessibility off, the guard simply does not sample).
- **Activity stream, real vs false drift, and leniency tiers (v1.98.0)** — the guard no longer judges a single frame: it reads a locally-built skeleton of your last 3 hours of activity (which app, doing what, for how long; stitched by a deterministic local algorithm at zero model cost, hard-capped at 500 tokens, rendered in recency layers). A false drift — the main line progressing well, a brief step aside to handle another important matter, back quickly — is effectively a temporary task switch and is let through; a real drift — the main line repeatedly interrupted, hopping around, even if every hop looks like business — still counts. Four leniency tiers follow: L3 (false drift, clear) never pops up and records nothing; L2 (false drift, likely) is a one-button minimal inquiry; L1 is the standard inquiry (and the default whenever the judge is unsure); L0 (real drift, clear) is a full stop — breathing by difficulty tier, one tap-to-answer reflection question, tightened graces. Two safeguards: the difficulty level you set while clear-headed is a pre-commitment floor the AI cannot pardon below, and a local hard guardrail (main-line share under 50% in the last hour, or two L0 verdicts within an hour) caps leniency at L1. **Stated plainly: the tiers fully take effect only once the server-side judging prompt that returns a leniency level is deployed; that deployment is still pending, and until then verdicts fall back to L1, the standard flow.**
- **Calendar panel** — a day view that opens inside the Schedule page beside the task list itself (the bottom tabs stay reachable): three lanes — a narrow Google Calendar lane (view-only reference; scheduling does not work around it), the day's plan lane (widest), and a narrow actual lane; drag tasks straight from the list into the calendar to plan them, long-press plan blocks to drag them to a new time, an "AI Re-plan" button that has the built-in AI draft a new plan for the rest of the day on the spot — it only lands when you tap Apply — and, since v1.27.0, a "Quick Re-plan" button beside it that re-plans instantly with a fixed algorithmic order (short due-and-important tasks first, then Must, due Important, due Secondary, not-due Important, not-due Secondary; Leisure never), leaving the running task, done blocks and hand-dragged blocks untouched — and, since v1.27.1, recycling an unfinished task's expired block so the task is re-planned from now instead of being stranded in the past — with a one-tap Undo. Both planners and the automatic gap-filling share one ordering implementation, and the AI prompt now also receives that same rule text, today's Google Calendar events (reference only) and what has already been done today. Blocks read in a fixed order: the task name first (up to two lines), then its priority marker — colour and shape only since v1.26.2 (solid vermilion for Must, an ink outline for Important, a small grey chip for Secondary, a dashed outline for Leisure) — then the start–end time as small grey print; short blocks drop the time before ever dropping the name.
- **Account and realtime sync (Supabase)** — sign in with an email account and tasks sync in realtime between devices; merges align by task id with newest-wins and tombstone deletes, so nothing resurrects and nothing is silently overwritten. There is a **single logical timer across devices**: state lives in the cloud, both ends render it, and either end can control it. A task written with an `autoStart` flag starts its own countdown as soon as it arrives. (The legacy Google Drive file bridge was retired and removed in v1.26.0; Supabase is the only sync path.)
- **Per-task alarms** — any task can have an alarm time (it rings whether or not a timer is running; a daily task re-arms for the next day) and/or ring when its timer ends, before the end-of-task check-in. The default sound is a clear nature recording that fades in from silence to full volume over 60 seconds, played on the alarm audio stream, so it rings even when the phone is muted.
- **Start-time reminders** — when a task's planned start time arrives, a full-screen reminder pops up: start now, snooze 10 minutes, or ignore.
- **Habits and the morning gate** — self-chosen habits in five goal types (since v1.82.0 they live in the Know tab's Precepts group, alongside the conduct principles) (at least N days a week, at most N times, a daily number, a cumulative total toward a goal, or — since v1.31.0 — a daily reminder: a passage you write to yourself), each with its own reminder time, a year grid for back-filling, and progress toward the week on every card; habits feed half of the Zhixing index on the Oneness tab. The day opens with a fullscreen morning gate: yesterday's habits answered one by one, a short read-only report on yesterday, and a morning diary, all before "Start today" unlocks the day. Since v1.28.0, a cumulative habit can carry a starting number, so a count begun before KnowDo continues from where it actually reached instead of from zero. Since v1.29.0, one global setting (Settings → Habit display window) chooses whether every card's main count reckons by today, this week, last week or this month. Since v1.30.0 the tab also has a compact mode — one line per habit with this week's seven dots and, on the right, how far the habit still is from this week's goal. A daily reminder's full text appears in the morning gate — yesterday's in the must-answer section, today's in a reading section — and tapping "Read" completes it for the day. Tapping the Zhixing index ring on the Oneness tab opens a trend page (v1.76.0): three switchable charts — the index itself, principle practice, and the "pure motive" share — by month or year, backed by per-day score records the app seals locally each day and syncs to the cloud.

## Design principles

1. **Meaning first.** Behaviour changes through meaning — let the user's own aspiration and principles persuade them; the software is meaning's messenger, not the master of their data. Being seen, friction and accountability are scaffolding, never the main lever.
2. **Two ledgers, one thing.** Knowing and doing are not two feature modules; detecting the gap and closing it is the product itself.
3. **Honest books.** A drift is a drift — graces, self-checks and appeals are all recorded truthfully. The counting rules are written as contracts and do not drift with UI redesigns; changing a goal never re-scores the past.
4. **Pre-commitment outranks in-the-moment judgement.** The difficulty level, aspiration and principles set while clear-headed are the floor; the AI may only tell real from false and grant leniency above that floor — it cannot pardon a real drift.
5. **Friction where it cuts.** What must be stopped is stopped fully (full-screen checks, an inquiry overlay that cannot be swiped away); what should pass, passes in one step. Every interruption must earn its attention.
6. **The privacy red line.** Original screenshots are never written to disk and never uploaded; only text events remain (sole exception: the user-enabled accountability mail, which sends only an in-memory mosaicked image to the user's own chosen recipient). Judging lets only text leave the phone, and providers may not retain it or train on it. Every account's data is isolated at the database level.
7. **Local first.** Offline, everything saves locally and queues; a network failure must never lose user input. Data made by hand (tasks, diaries, habits, principles) has the highest priority and is never deleted by time.
8. **The AI proposes, the human decides.** An AI re-plan lands only when you tap Apply; an AI task stops before the final irreversible step; AI changes to settings or deletions always go through a proposal card first.
9. **No punishment, only reckoning.** No money fines, no shaming publicity. Nagging has tiers, mute has exits, and how strict things get is the user's own choice.
10. **Eastern minimalism.** Rice-paper off-white, ink blue-black, one touch of vermilion; large, sparse type and generous whitespace — the interface itself should quiet you down, not become one more anxiety-making efficiency tool.
11. **Open and continuable.** CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; derivatives must stay free and non-commercial; data formats stay stable across implementations, so changing implementations never loses the books.

## For experts: the behaviour-change design

*This section is for readers who study behaviour change; everyday use does not need it.*

KnowDo's basic assumption is that white-knuckling it in the moment is unreliable, so the structure is moved outside: absolute end times, the no-gap three states, the morning gate, one logical timer across devices — the environment keeps the rules so the user does not have to be disciplined every minute. (The author once maintained focus for years with paid human supervision; this app is the latest generation of that strategy.) Difficulty levels, the aspiration, principles and pause purposes are pre-commitments written while clear-headed; in the moment, the AI may only grant leniency above their floor. The unit of drift judgement is upgraded from a single act to a 3-hour behaviour pattern (see the leniency feature above). The Atomic Habits mapping is deliberate: the morning gate is “make it obvious” with a fixed trigger and habits stacked into the morning flow; tonight's line is an if-then implementation intention; the index and year grids are visible progress feedback; the ever-present aspiration is identity — become this kind of person first, then count the doing. Deliberately absent: broken-streak shaming and fines. Feedback reconciles; it does not punish.

## For experts: the Yangming mapping

*This section is for readers with a Confucian, Buddhist or Daoist practice background; everyday use does not need it. Quotations were checked against the Chuanxilu (传习录).*

Yangming: “There has never been knowing without doing; knowing without doing is simply not yet knowing,” and “Knowing is the beginning of doing; doing is the completion of knowing.” Knowing and doing fall into two halves only because selfish desire cuts them apart — so the gaps KnowDo records are never knowledge gaps; they are counts of the cut: drifts, “I wasn't” self-checks, “no” answers at the motive gate. The aspiration appears only at the four moments most easily cut off — Yangming's “if the aspiration is not established, nothing under heaven can be accomplished” (《教条示龙场诸生》, not the Chuanxilu) as a product decision. On the completion page, “didn't consider it” withdraws the principle's link: the product form of “simply not yet knowing”. The software only poses the question; the verdict belongs to the user's own innate knowledge — KnowDo does not extend your innate knowledge for you, and the index is a reckoning instrument, not a “quantified unity score”. Two borrowings are refused here: the morning page is gathering the heart plus daily reflection, not seated meditation (Yangming calls quiet-sitting a beginner's expedient only); and AI principle-matching runs against “innate knowledge needs no outside seeking” — it is an external index and reminder tool, nothing more. The honest gaps: the conscience layer has no feature yet by the current ruling, and the one-thought journal (a later development of Liu Zongzhou's school, not Yangming's own teaching) is not built — reflection today leans on self-checks produced when the guard catches you, so the battlefield is still behaviour, not yet thought.

## Built with multiple AI agents

KnowDo is not written by one pair of hands. The author (Ti Guo) is the product owner and final arbiter. Muse serves as his secretary for life affairs, reviews every pull request, and owns versions and releases. The Grok team — a manager coordinating, a dedicated KnowDo coder — writes most of the feature code. Collaboration runs through the Agent Guild, a private repository where every topic is one Issue, comments stay short, long material goes into docs and is linked, and no private information ever enters the repo.

The engineering discipline is written into the repo's AGENTS.md and binds every agent: every major feature ships as its own version; a serious task list comes before any work; six red-line files hold the counting and semantics contracts (the Zhixing index, the time account, self-checks, semantic judging, habit data, the logical day boundary) and stay at zero diff unless the author personally approves; commits include files by name, never a blanket add; bug-fix releases must actually run in an Android emulator before anyone may call them fixed (and when the emulator cannot run, the report says so plainly); every small bug is treated as a small feature — find the systemic problem and fix that, never patch a single hole; delivery reports separate three layers: personally verified (and how), reported by the developer, and not yet verified.

Architecturally: native Android Java/Views (one small Kotlin bridge for Health Connect), layered as TaskStore (data) / sync layer / TimerService (timing) / UI, so data formats stay stable across implementations; Supabase realtime sync with a single logical timer across devices; a hybrid guard pipeline (on-device pre-screening, a cloud small-model final judge over text only — no retention, no training — and a local fallback offline); and a self-update channel of verified ~5 MB shards. The Mac companion shares the same account, task list and logical timer.

## What's next

*Everything in this section is direction only — not yet built, and not promised on any date.*

Next, KnowDo wants the AI in Chat to feel more like a companion living inside the app: able to speak first at the right moment (for example, asking at a fixed time each evening what time you plan to sleep tonight — your answer becomes tonight's commitment, reckoned the next day), and able to bring the right page in front of you to talk things over when a task is about to miss its time. Further out: “how to think and how to act in a given situation” packaged as training plans you can switch on or off — switch one on, and the AI practises it with you. Furthest out: a built-in AI that can watch your activity stream, prompt you when a prompt is due, and chat with you about it. None of this is implemented; when it is, it will appear in the changelog.

## For new users: what the AI can and cannot do

KnowDo's AI comes in two parts, and the difference matters:

- **The built-in chat works out of the box.** It runs on the project's free model channel, and every AI button inside the app uses this same built-in chatbot for all users — there is no special channel for the author; in the app, the author uses the same AI as everyone else. The calendar's "AI Re-plan" is built-in too: it drafts a new plan on the spot, and the plan only lands when you tap Apply.
- **The patrol features are the author's personal AI setup, not app features.** Filing tasks dictated in a conversation outside the app, watching the cloud for a chance to re-plan, and classifying per-task app lists are done by the author's own assistant, patrolling the author's own cloud account in the background. For new users, bluntly: the built-in chat is the one and only AI you can connect to — there is no other option, and there is currently no way to connect your own external assistant. It cannot enter this app's cloud database, and no patrol is running for you. App-list classification is the same story: lists are generated in the background for the author's account only; other accounts, for now, have no app lists, and use local auto-planning, on-device guard verdicts, and the built-in chat.
- **Your data is isolated per account at the database level.** Your tasks, lists and records live only under your own account. The author cannot access them, and neither can the author's assistant; nobody's AI can read anyone else's data. That is enforced by the database, not by a verbal promise.

**New**

## Build

Requires JDK 17 and Android SDK 34 (Gradle 8.7 + AGP 8.5.2):

```sh
./build-gradle.sh
```

The APK lands in `app/build/outputs/apk/release/app-release.apk`. Signing uses a local debug keystore, so new builds install over old ones. `./build-manual.sh` is the retired zero-dependency pipeline from the v1.3.x era, kept for history only; it cannot build current sources.

Cloud sync reads a Supabase **publishable** key at build time from a local, untracked `supabase_public_key.txt` (never committed). Without it, the app still builds and runs with local tasks.
